## 1.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1. In an era marked by rapid technological automation, industrial supply-chain shifts, post-LDC trade preference adjustments, and periodic macroeconomic shocks, economic displacement is an inherent reality of a dynamic labor market. While Bangladesh operates public works safety nets such as the **Employment Generation Programme for the Poorest (EGPP)** providing seasonal manual labor for the rural extreme poor, the country possesses **zero institutional social insurance protection against involuntary unemployment** for formal, industrial, and skilled service workers.
2. The existing statutory mechanism for managing layoffs—**employer-paid retrenchment severance** under Chapter II of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Section 20)**—mandates 30 days of wages per year of service. When enterprises face sudden market liquidation, factory closures, or bankruptcy, employers almost universally default on these severance payments. This leads to bitter industrial disputes, wildcat strikes, road blockades, and acute humanitarian crises among retrenched workers.
3. Under the **National Social Security Strategy (NSSS)**, the Government of Bangladesh committed to creating an integrated **Unemployment Insurance Scheme** combined with active re-employment services. This policy document formally establishes the **National Unemployment Insurance and Active Re-Employment Scheme (NUI-ARES)**, fully aligned with **ILO Convention No. 168 (Employment Promotion and Protection against Unemployment Convention, 1988)** and **ILO Convention No. 102 (Part IV: Unemployment Benefit)**.
4. NUI-ARES does not function merely as a passive cash-handout system. It operates on the modern principle of **Mutual Obligation**:
   - **Passive Income Replacement:** Delivers tiered, time-bound monthly cash benefits replacing 50% to 70% of previous insured earnings for 3 to 6 months to prevent catastrophic consumption collapse.
   - **Active Labour Market Policies (ALMPs):** Integrates mandatory job-search reporting, computerized job-matching, fully funded technical reskilling via the **National Skills Development Authority (NSDA)**, and early re-employment cash incentives.
5. In accordance with government policy standards where national enterprise retrenchment microdata is still being collected, this policy document provides a finalized statutory and operational framework. All calculative fields (claim incidence %, replacement rates, duration months, service vouchers, contribution rates %, and reserve multipliers) are marked with **standardized bracketed placeholders** containing exact data acquisition instructions.
6. A comprehensive audit of existing non-contributory workfare and safety net programs in Bangladesh (such as EGPP and donor-backed COVID-19 relief initiatives) is provided in the final dedicated section.

## 2.0 POLICY CONTEXT, LEGAL BASIS & STRATEGIC RATIONALE

### 2.1 Constitutional Anchor
This policy directly operationalizes **Article 15(d) of the Constitution of the People's Republic of Bangladesh**, which guarantees:
> *"The right to public assistance in cases of undeserved want arising from unemployment..."*

Furthermore, **Article 15(b)** affirms the fundamental state responsibility to guarantee:
> *"The right to work, that is the right to guaranteed employment at a reasonable wage, having regard to the quantity and quality of work."*

### 2.2 Alignment with National Economic Priorities
1. **National Social Security Strategy (NSSS) Action Plan:** Explicitly mandates the introduction of a contributory Unemployment Insurance Scheme under the NSIS window.
2. **Post-LDC Graduation Industrial Transition:** As Bangladesh graduates from LDC status and tariffs adjust, export sectors (RMG, leather, agro-processing) will undergo industrial restructuring. NUI-ARES provides the necessary social shock-absorber, protecting workers during enterprise transitions while maintaining labor peace.
3. **4th Industrial Revolution (4IR) & Automation:** Automation in textiles, sewing, and assembly lines will displace repetitive labor. NUI-ARES finances the reskilling of displaced workers into higher-productivity technical trades.

### 2.3 The Failure of Employer-Paid Severance Pay
Under Section 20 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006:
- **Bankruptcy Immunity:** Insolvent factories abandon production sites without paying legally owed severance.
- **Unfunded Liability:** Enterprises rarely hold segregated, ring-fenced bank reserves for severance liabilities, treating termination costs as unplanned operational losses.
- **Litigation Deadlocks:** Disputed retrenchment claims languish in Labour Courts for years, during which destitute workers cannot afford legal representation.
- **Absence of Job Matching:** Severance pay offers zero assistance in retraining or finding a new job, leaving workers stranded in long-term structural unemployment.

### 7.1 Apex Tripartite Governance
The scheme operates under the **National Employment and Social Insurance Board (NESIB)**, chaired by the Secretary of MoLE, featuring executive representation from the Ministry of Finance, National Skills Development Authority (NSDA), Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training (BMET), FBCCI, BGMEA, and national trade union federations.

### 7.2 The National Employment Service (NES) Digital Platform
- A unified web and mobile platform connecting displaced workers with job vacancies nationwide.
- Uses artificial intelligence to match worker resumes, verified skills, and geographical preferences with employer job postings.
- Integrates with BMET and technical training institutes to issue automatic training vouchers upon claim approval.

### 7.3 Anti-Fraud & Integrity Mechanisms
- **Automated Payroll Linkage:** Cross-matches claims against real-time corporate tax filings and monthly NSIS contribution reports to detect unrecorded re-employment.
- **Biometric Check-Ins:** Claimants complete monthly biometric check-ins (via NID-linked smartphone app or local union digital center) to confirm continued active job searching.

### 8.1 Detailed Inventory of Existing Schemes in Bangladesh

#### 1. Employment Generation Programme for the Poorest (EGPP)
- **Legal & Policy Anchor:** Tax-financed public workfare scheme administered by the Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief (MoDMR) / Department of Disaster Management (DDM); anchored in NSSS Working-Age Pillar.
- **Official FY2026-27 Figures (Finance Division Social Security Budget Report):**
  - **Daily Wage:** **Tk 500 per day** (Tk 550 for a Sardar/supervisor).
  - **Employment Guarantee:** **Two 40-day cycles = 80 days total** of manual labor during agricultural lean seasons (October–December and March–April).
  - **Beneficiary Target:** **3.70 lakh (370,000)** extreme-poor rural manual laborers.
  - **Annual Budget Allocation:** **Tk 1,632.84 crore**.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *Inapplicable to Industrial Workers:* EGPP requires manual earth-moving and rural canal excavation. A skilled sewing machine operator, textile technician, or commercial clerk cannot perform seasonal rural earthwork.
  - *No Skills Formation:* Provides basic survival wages without offering vocational training or pathways back into formal productivity.

#### 2. Employer-Paid Severance & Retrenchment Pay (Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Chapter II, Sections 20–27)
- **Legal Character:** Direct individual enterprise liability.
- **Statutory Schedule:**
  - **Retrenchment (Section 20):** One month’s notice in writing (or wages in lieu thereof) plus compensation equal to **30 days of wages for every completed year of service**, or gratuity, whichever is higher.
  - **Discharge (Section 22):** Compensation equal to 30 days wages per completed year of service for certified physical/mental incapacity.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *Widespread Default in Enterprise Liquidation:* If an export factory suddenly shuts down due to lost buyer orders or bankruptcy, owners frequently abscond or enter receivership, leaving thousands of workers with zero severance pay.
  - *Industrial Unrest:* Unpaid severance is the primary cause of industrial road blockades, garment worker protests, and factory property damage.
  - *Discourages Enterprise Restructuring:* Viable firms needing to adjust workforce size to survive technology shifts are paralyzed by massive cash severance liabilities.

#### 3. Unemployed Workers' Protection Programme (UWPP) — Donor-Backed Emergency Relief (2020–2022)
- **Context & Operation:** Introduced during the COVID-19 pandemic to mitigate the impact of mass layoffs across export-oriented garment and leather sectors.
- **Financing:** Funded entirely by international development grants: **European Union (EUR 93 million / ~Tk 930 crore)** and the **German Government through KfW (EUR 20 million / ~Tk 200 crore)**.
- **Benefit Disbursal:** Disbursed an emergency cash grant of **Tk 3,000 per month for up to 3 months** directly through MFS wallets (bKash/Nagad) to verified laid-off RMG workers.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *Donor Dependency & Termination:* The initiative was an emergency response; when donor grants were exhausted, the program ceased entirely.
  - *Absence of Permanent Architecture:* Proved the feasibility of digital payments to retrenched workers, but highlighted the critical failure of not establishing a permanent, self-financed domestic social insurance framework.

#### 4. National Service Programme (NSP)
- **Agency:** Ministry of Youth and Sports / Department of Youth Development.
- **Objective:** Temporary attachment of educated, unemployed youth (HSC and above) to local government bodies for 2 years following 3 months of basic training.
- **Benefit:** Daily allowance of Tk 100 during training, Tk 200 during attachment (with Tk 50 deposited into mandatory savings).
- **Structural Deficiencies:** A temporary youth placement scheme with limited geographic reach; cannot serve as an industrial unemployment insurance mechanism.

### 8.3 Strategic Imperative for Policy Enactment
The lack of unemployment insurance is the single greatest structural vulnerability in Bangladesh's industrial economy. By enacting the **National Unemployment Insurance and Active Re-Employment Scheme (NUI-ARES)**, Bangladesh replaces industrial strikes, bankruptcy defaults, and poverty distress with a modern, self-financing institutional framework that guarantees income security, accelerates re-employment, and protects the dignity of the nation's working men and women.

## 1.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1. Female labor force participation has been the central engine of Bangladesh’s economic transformation, particularly across the readymade garment (RMG) sector, pharmaceuticals, education, healthcare, and financial services. However, reproductive rights and maternal health remain severely undermined by the legacy compensation mechanism: **direct individual employer liability** under Chapter IV of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (as amended)**.
2. Under current law, private employers are individually liable to pay 120 days (~17.14 weeks) of full wages, while public sector institutions and progressive multinational companies provide 6 months (24–26 weeks) of maternity leave. Placing 100% of this direct cash cost onto individual enterprise payrolls creates a severe **gender penalty**: it incentivizes discriminatory hiring practices, unrecorded pregnancies, unlawful terminations, and reluctance to promote female workers.
3. This policy establishes the **National Maternity Protection and Insurance Scheme (NMPIS)** under the **National Social Security Strategy (NSSS)**, fully aligned with **ILO Convention No. 183 (Maternity Protection Convention, 2000)** and **ILO Convention No. 102**.
4. The NMPIS introduces a comprehensive two-tier reform:
   - **Tier 1 (National Risk-Pooling of Core Maternity Wages):** Replaces individual employer liability with a collective social insurance fund. Employers no longer pay maternity cash wages out of enterprise cash flow; instead, the national pooled fund replaces 100% of wages, eliminating the financial penalty for hiring female workers.
   - **Tier 2 (Extended Social Insurance Benefits — Additional to Company-Provided 6 Months):** For formal enterprises already providing 6 months of maternity leave, the social insurance scheme provides **supplementary protections beyond the 6-month window**, including:
     - Extended Childcare & Post-Maternity Bridging Cash Allowances (up to an additional 8 to 12 weeks of subsidized leave);
     - Cashless Comprehensive Obstetric Healthcare Grants (covering prenatal, delivery, and postnatal medical complications);
     - Nutritional Lactation Stipends paid directly to mothers for the first 6 months following return to work to prevent stunting and support exclusive breastfeeding;
     - Subsidized social insurance windows for informal, domestic, and gig-economy female workers.
5. All quantitative parameters (birth rates, contribution rates, wage ceilings, medical allowances, and reserve ratios) are marked with **standardized bracketed placeholders** accompanied by rigorous actuarial data collection instructions. A comprehensive audit of Bangladesh's existing non-contributory social assistance schemes (such as the Mother and Child Benefit Programme - MCBP) is provided in the final section.

## 2.0 POLICY CONTEXT, LEGAL BASIS & STRATEGIC RATIONALE

### 2.1 Constitutional Anchor
This policy gives practical statutory effect to:
- **Article 15(d) of the Constitution of Bangladesh:** Guaranteeing social security support in motherhood, illness, and undeserved want.
- **Article 27 & 28(2):** Enshrining equality of all citizens before the law and guaranteeing women equal rights with men in all spheres of the State and public life.
- **Article 28(4):** Explicitly empowering the State to make *special provisions in favour of women and children*.

### 2.2 International Normative Compliance (ILO C183 Standard)
Article 6, Paragraph 8 of **ILO Convention No. 183** explicitly mandates:
> *"In order to protect the situation of women in the labour market, benefits in respect of the leave shall be provided through compulsory social insurance or public funds, or in a manner determined by national law and practice. An employer shall not be individually liable for the direct cost of any such monetary benefit to a woman employed by him or her without that employer's specific agreement..."*

Bangladesh’s current statutory framework directly violates this international principle by placing the direct financial liability upon individual employers, depressing formal female employment.

### 2.3 The Economic Imperative: Overcoming the Female Labor Trap
- **The Hiring Disincentive:** Enterprises calculate that hiring a female worker between ages 20–35 carries an unhedged contingent liability of 4 to 6 months of unearned salary plus lost productivity.
- **Premature Career Exit:** Over 35% of skilled female factory and service workers permanently leave the workforce after childbirth due to lack of medical cost coverage, absent childcare infrastructure, and zero financial support during extended infant care.
- **Demographic Dividend & Child Stunting:** Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months and optimal nutrition during the first 1,000 days of life require financial security so that mothers are not forced into desperate premature manual labor.

## 3.0 INTERNATIONAL BENCHMARKING & COMPARATIVE SOCIAL SECURITY MODELS

To establish a world-class policy structure, four relevant international maternity models were benchmarked:

| Country | System & Governing Entity | Duration & Financing Architecture | Extended / Additional Benefits Beyond Core Leave | Health & Childcare Integration | Key Lessons for Bangladesh |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vietnam** | **Vietnam Social Insurance (VSI)** under Law on Social Insurance | **6 full months (26 weeks)** paid at 100% of average insurable wage. Financed via pooled 3% payroll contribution (split across Sickness & Maternity). | Additional leave for multiple births (+1 month per additional child); miscarriage leave (up to 50 days); 30 days post-maternity convalescence leave. | One-time childbirth grant equal to 2 months of statutory base salary per child; comprehensive prenatal exam vouchers. | Demonstrates that developing Asian manufacturing economies can successfully finance 6 months of paid maternity leave through collective social insurance without damaging industrial competitiveness. |
| **Singapore** | **Government-Paid Maternity Leave (GPML)** / Ministry of Manpower | **16 weeks total:** First 8 weeks funded by employer; **9th to 16th weeks funded 100% by Government/Tax Pool** (up to SGD 10,000 per 4-week block). | Second and subsequent births: Government pays **all 16 weeks** of maternity leave. - **Cash Benefit Formula:**

### 5.1 Equity-Based Contribution Base (Rejecting Female-Only Payroll Taxes)
- **Policy Stance:** In the simplified scenario calculator (`script.js`), an illustrative scenario charges contributions strictly against female-worker payroll (`getContributionBase = workers * femaleShare * wage * 12`).

Administered by the **National Maternity Social Insurance Commission (NMSIC)**, co-chaired by the Secretary of MoLE and the Secretary of MoWCA, with executive representation from the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS), FBCCI, BGMEA, and female trade union leaders.

- Upon confirmation of pregnancy by an accredited doctor, the female worker receives a digital **Matri-Passbook** linked to her NID.
- Tracks prenatal check-ups, certifies expected delivery date (EDD), triggers automatic leave authorization to the employer, and initiates direct payment of obstetric grants.

- **Absolute Dismissal Prohibition:** Any termination, demotion, or wage reduction of a female worker from the date of pregnancy notification until `[PLACEHOLDER: Protected Period Post-Delivery - Instruction: Recommended 12 months following delivery]` is illegal, punishable by heavy statutory fines and compulsory reinstatement with back pay.
- **Mandatory Nursing Breaks:** Employers must provide two paid 30-minute lactation breaks per working day in clean, private on-site nursing facilities (Labour Act Sec 94A).

### 7.1 Detailed Inventory of Existing Schemes in Bangladesh

#### 1. Employer-Liability Maternity Benefit (Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, Chapter IV, Sections 45–50)
- **Legal Character:** Direct statutory liability imposed on the individual employer.
- **Mandated Duration:** **120 days** (extended from earlier 16 weeks / 112 days under recent amendments; flexible distribution before and after delivery).
- **Benefit Level:** Full average wage based on earnings during the 3 months preceding the leave.
- **Qualifying Condition:** At least 6 months of employment with the same employer prior to delivery; benefit restricted to women with fewer than two surviving children.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *The Hiring Penalty:* Induces systemic employer avoidance of female job applicants in childbearing age brackets.
  - *Coerced Resignations:* Unscrupulous enterprises frequently fabricate disciplinary violations or terminate pregnant workers before they achieve the 6-month qualifying threshold.
  - *Insolvency Vulnerability:* If a factory faces liquidity crises, maternity wages are often delayed or entirely withheld.
  - *Exclusion of Healthcare Costs:* The law covers wages only; it provides zero coverage for clinical delivery, prenatal complications, or emergency surgical interventions.

#### 2. Mother and Child Benefit Programme (MCBP)
- **Legal Character:** Tax-financed, non-contributory cash transfer under the Ministry of Women and Children Affairs (MoWCA) / Department of Women Affairs (DWA).
- **Official FY2026-27 Figures (Finance Division Budget Report):**
  - **Benefit Amount:** **Tk 850 per month**, disbursed quarterly via mobile financial services (bKash/Nagad).
  - **Coverage / Beneficiary Target:** **18.95 lakh (1.895 million)** pregnant and lactating mothers and infants up to 4 years of age.
  - **Annual Budget Allocation:** **Tk 1,968.20 crore**.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *Poverty Transfer, Not Wage Replacement:* Tk 850/month covers barely 5% to 6% of an industrial wage (Tk 15,000/month). It prevents extreme destitution but cannot smooth income for wage-earning workers.
  - *Targeting Exclusions:* Excludes formal wage-earners by design due to poverty-targeting filters.

#### 3. Vulnerable Women Benefit (VWB) Programme
- **Legal Character:** Tax-financed food security and economic inclusion transfer (formerly Vulnerable Group Development - VGD).
- **Official FY2026-27 Figures:**
  - **Benefit Amount:** **30 kg of fortified rice per month** per beneficiary household, coupled with life-skills and entrepreneurship training.
  - **Coverage / Beneficiary Target:** **10.40 lakh (1.04 million)** ultra-poor women.
  - **Annual Budget Allocation:** **Tk 2,297.70 crore**.
- **Structural Deficiencies:** An in-kind nutrition safety net targeting chronically poor, distressed women. It is not an employment-linked social protection instrument.

#### 4. Public Sector Paid Maternity Leave (Government Civil Service)
- **Legal Character:** Government service condition financed directly from state revenue.
- **Duration & Benefit:** **6 months (26 weeks) fully paid leave** for female civil servants (limited to two surviving children).
- **Structural Deficiencies:** Confined strictly to the ~1.5 million civil servants and public autonomous agency employees. Creates a severe inequity between public sector employees (who enjoy 6 months paid leave) and private sector female workers (who receive only 120 days under individual employer liability).

#### 5. Family Card Programme
- **Legal Character:** Tax-financed household cash transfer under the Ministry of Social Welfare.
- **Official FY2026-27 Figures:** **Tk 2,500 per month** targeting **41 lakh poor households** with a public budget of **Tk 14,500 crore**, generally registered in the female head's or mother's name.

### 7.3 Strategic Conclusion & Policy Direction
Continuing with direct employer liability places an unfair burden on female workers and penalizes ethical employers. Establishing the **National Maternity Protection and Insurance Scheme (NMPIS)** is the single most effective legal and institutional reform to achieve gender equality, boost formal female labor participation, and secure the health of future generations in Bangladesh.

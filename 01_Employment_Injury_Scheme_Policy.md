## 1.0 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1. Every year, thousands of Bangladeshi workers suffer occupational accidents, acute physical impairments, fatal factory mishaps, and debilitating occupational diseases. The existing compensation regime—rooted in Chapter XII of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (as amended)**—relies on direct individual employer liability. This legacy framework mandates modest, one-off lump-sum settlements (e.g., maximum Tk 2,50,000 for fatal injury and Tk 3,00,000 for total permanent disability). In practice, this creates an unviable double burden: workers and bereaved families fall into catastrophic generational poverty, while catastrophic industrial events expose individual employers to sudden, unmanageable balance-sheet liabilities.
2. Under the **National Social Security Strategy (NSSS)** and its Action Plans, the Government of the People's Republic of Bangladesh committed to establishing a modern, rights-based **National Social Insurance Scheme (NSIS)**. The **National Employment Injury Insurance Scheme (NEIIS)** represents the foundational operational pillar of this reform. 
3. NEIIS establishes a **no-fault, employer-financed, collective risk-pooling social insurance system** fully harmonized with **ILO Convention No. 121 (Employment Injury Benefits Convention, 1964)** and **ILO Convention No. 102 (Social Security Minimum Standards Convention, 1952)**. 
4. The scheme covers four core contingencies:
   - Comprehensive curative and rehabilitative medical care;
   - Temporary Disability Cash Benefits (periodic income replacement during sick leaves);
   - Permanent Disability Monthly Pensions (actuarially calculated lifetime pensions indexed to inflation);
   - Survivors’ Monthly Pensions and funeral grants for dependents of deceased workers.
5. In accordance with state policy guidelines where complete national baseline microdata is still being collected, this policy document establishes a textually definitive legal and operational structure. All calculative fields (actuarial contribution rates, monetary benefit ceilings, waiting periods, capitalization reserves, and risk-differentiation multipliers) are clearly demarcated with **standardized bracketed placeholders** containing exact methodological instructions for actuarial valuation teams and administrative data custodians.
6. A dedicated comparative section at the end of this document thoroughly audits existing non-contributory, assistance-based, and employer-liability programs currently operating across Bangladesh, establishing the economic and moral justification for this transition.

## 2.0 POLICY CONTEXT, LEGAL BASIS & STRATEGIC RATIONALE

### 2.1 Constitutional Anchor
This policy directly operationalizes **Article 15(d) of the Constitution of the People's Republic of Bangladesh**, which guarantees the right to social security:
> *"The provision of the basic necessities of life... the right to public assistance in cases of undeserved want arising from unemployment, illness or disablement, or sufferance by widowhood or orphanhood, or in old age, or in other such cases."*

Furthermore, **Article 20(1)** enshrines work as a right and a matter of honour for every citizen, imposing a reciprocal state obligation to ensure safe, dignified, and socially insured working conditions.

### 2.2 National Strategic Alignment
The scheme forms a core deliverable under:
1. **National Social Security Strategy (NSSS) Action Plan (Phase II: 2021–2026 / 2026–2030 Roadmap):** Mandates consolidating social protection programs and creating a contributory social insurance framework for the working-age population.
2. **MoLE Tripartite Strategic Compact & Decent Work Country Programme (DWCP):** Fulfills international trade covenants, ensuring export competitiveness following Bangladesh's official graduation from Least Developed Country (LDC) status.

### 2.3 The Failure of Employer Liability
Under direct employer liability (Labour Act 2006, Chapter XII):
- **High Insolvency & Default Risk:** If an enterprise closes, burns down, or enters bankruptcy following a disaster, victims cannot recover legally owed lump sums.
- **Inadequacy of Lump Sums:** Lump-sum awards are rapidly depleted on emergency healthcare, debt repayment, and living costs within 12–24 months, leaving permanent paraplegics, amputees, and orphaned children destitute.
- **Litigiousness and Friction:** Injured workers must pursue adversarial legal battles through the Labour Courts, causing multi-year delays and severe financial strain.
- **Absence of Occupational Rehabilitation:** Individual employers possess neither the clinical infrastructure nor the economic incentive to provide long-term physical, prosthetic, and vocational retraining.

## 4.0 SCOPE OF COVERAGE & PHASED IMPLEMENTATION STRATEGY

### 4.1 Target Population
NEIIS covers all wage-employed persons suffering injuries, fatalities, or certified occupational illnesses arising out of and in the course of employment, including commuting accidents along direct work travel corridors.

### 4.2 Phased Rollout Schedule
To guarantee institutional stability, the scheme shall expand across three progressive phases:

```
[Phase 1: 2026-2028]  --> RMG & Export Sectors (Formalize EIS Pilot, ~4.5 Million Workers)

[Phase 3: 2032 Onward] --> Micro-enterprises, Informal-sector wage earners, Domestic & Agricultural Workers
```

## 5.0 CONTINGENCIES COVERED & QUALIFYING CONDITIONS

### 5.1 Compensable Events
The scheme provides immediate, no-fault coverage for:
1. **Workplace Accidents:** Physical injuries, trauma, or deaths occurring at the work premises, job site, or official off-site assignments.
2. **Commuting Accidents:** Accidents occurring on direct routes between the worker's official residence and the workplace, without unapproved personal detours.
3. **Occupational Diseases:** Pathologies, chronic toxicities, and physical impairments caused by exposure to chemical, biological, or ergonomic workplace hazards, as codified in the **National Schedule of Prescribed Occupational Diseases (Schedule-A)**.

### 5.2 Qualifying Conditions (No Waiting Period)
- **Zero Minimum Contribution Threshold:** Unlike pensions or unemployment insurance, employment injury coverage attaches **from the first hour of employment**. A worker who suffers an occupational accident on their first day of work is entitled to full medical care and income benefits.
- **Reporting Timelines:** Employers must notify the NEIIS Claims Management Office and DIFE within `[PLACEHOLDER: Statutory Accident Reporting Window - Instruction: Set by Tripartite Rules; recommended 48 hours for serious injury/fatality, 7 days for minor injury]` of incident occurrence.

The scheme is administered by an autonomous statutory body under the Ministry of Labour and Employment:
- **Composition:** Equal tripartite representation (Government Ministries: MoLE, MoF, MoSW, MoHFW; Employers: FBCCI, BGMEA, BKMEA, Sectoral Associations; Workers: National Tripartite Trade Union Federations).
- **Core Powers:** Approves annual actuarial valuations, determines medical provider fee schedules, audits investments, and monitors OSH prevention outcomes.

### 8.2 National Medical Assessment Board (NMAB)
- Composed of certified occupational health physicians, orthopedic surgeons, neurologists, and rehabilitation specialists.
- Conducts standardized whole-person impairment examinations within `[PLACEHOLDER: Impairment Assessment Timeline - Instruction: Standard maximum 30 days from medical stability (plateau) date]` of clinical stabilization.

- **Unified Employer/Worker Registry:** Linked directly to the **National ID (NID)** system, factory registration numbers (DIFE), and Corporate Tax Identification Numbers (e-TIN).

- **First-Tier Administrative Review:** Independent Grievance Redress Officer within 15 days of notice.
- **Second-Tier Social Insurance Appeals Tribunal:** Independent quasi-judicial panel comprising a judicial magistrate, occupational physician, and tripartite representative. Decisions rendered within 60 days.

### 11.1 Detailed Inventory of Existing Schemes in Bangladesh

#### 1. Employer Liability under Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006 (Chapter XII, Sections 150–174)
- **Legal Character:** Direct individual employer liability (fault-based or strict liability under statute).
- **Statutory Benefit Schedule:**
  - **Fatal Accident:** One-off lump-sum compensation of **Tk 2,50,000** (increased from earlier Tk 1,00,000).
  - **Permanent Total Disability:** One-off lump-sum compensation of **Tk 3,00,000** (increased from earlier Tk 1,25,000).
  - **Temporary Incapacity:** Full wages for first two months, two-thirds of wages for next two months, and half wages for subsequent months up to a maximum of one year.
- **Financing:** Unfunded. The employer pays directly out of enterprise working capital or cash flows.
- **Institutional Custodian:** Labour Courts under the Ministry of Labour and Employment.
- **Structural Deficiencies:**
  - *Inadequacy:* Tk 2,50,000 represents less than 17 months of an average worker's wage (Tk 15,000/month), leaving a permanently disabled 25-year-old worker or surviving family destitute for decades.
  - *Default in Enterprise Failure:* In the event of catastrophic fires, collapses, or business insolvency (e.g., Tazreen Fashion, Rana Plaza), employers cannot pay, forcing victims to rely on charity.
  - *Adversarial Nature:* Disputed cases remain backlogged in Labour Courts for years.

#### 2. The Central Fund for the RMG Sector (Section 199A of the Bangladesh Labour Act)
- **Legal Character:** Earmarked sector-specific social fund established under MoLE.
- **Financing Source:** Financed by an automatic levy of **0.03% of the free-on-board (FOB) export value** of all readymade garment exports, deducted directly through commercial banks.
- **Benefits Provided:**
  - Discretionary fatal injury / natural death grant: Maximum **Tk 2,00,000** to the deceased worker’s family.
  - Medical assistance grant for serious illness/injury: Up to **Tk 1,00,000**.
  - Educational stipends for workers' children.
- **Annual Public Allocation:** Non-budgetary sector fund; annual collections fluctuate between Tk 150 crore to Tk 220 crore.
- **Structural Deficiencies:** Discretionary one-time grants rather than periodic income replacement; restricted solely to the RMG sector; lacks clinical rehabilitation and lifetime pension capacity.

#### 3. Bangladesh Workers Welfare Foundation (BWWF) Fund
- **Legal Character:** Statutory welfare fund under the Bangladesh Workers Welfare Foundation Act, 2006.
- **Financing Source:** 5% of net profits allocated by participating formal corporations (Section 234 of Labour Act: 80% to Participation Fund, 10% to Welfare Fund, 10% to BWWF Fund).
- **Benefits Provided:** One-time emergency medical grants (up to Tk 1,00,000) and death relief grants (up to Tk 2,00,000) for workers across non-RMG sectors.
- **Structural Deficiencies:** Low corporate compliance outside large multinationals and major domestic conglomerates; highly discretionary; cannot deliver actuarially sustained periodic pensions.

#### 4. The RMG Employment Injury Scheme (EIS) Pilot (2022–Present)
- **Legal Character:** Tripartite Pilot Framework established by MoLE, BGMEA, BKMEA, and the Central Fund, with technical support from ILO and GIZ.
- **Target Beneficiaries:** Approximately 4.0 million workers in the export-oriented RMG sector.
- **Financing Mechanism:** Voluntary contributions by international apparel brands and buyers equal to **0.019% of annual export value**, supplemented by donor technical assistance. **No statutory domestic employer payroll premium is currently in force.**
- **Benefit Innovation:**
  - Tops up the existing statutory lump sum under the Labour Act to provide an **ILO Convention No. 121-compliant monthly lifetime pension** for permanent disability and survivors.
  - Monthly disability pension benchmark: At least **60% of former gross wage** multiplied by assessed disability percentage.
  - Survivors' pension benchmark: At least **50% of former gross wage**.
- **Structural Deficiencies:** Voluntary, temporary pilot status. Relying on voluntary brand contributions is precarious and unsustainable without a gazetted national legal mandate covering all domestic employers.

#### 5. National Disability Allowance Programme (Insolvent Persons with Disabilities)
- **Legal Character:** Tax-financed, non-contributory social assistance cash transfer.
- **Responsible Agency:** Ministry of Social Welfare (MoSW) / Department of Social Services (DSS).
- **Benefit Level:** **Tk 1,000 per month** (FY2026-27 official budget allocation: **Tk 4,715.45 crore** covering approximately **39 lakh (3.9 million)** disabled citizens).
- **Structural Deficiencies:** Designed as a poverty relief transfer, not income replacement. A cash transfer of Tk 1,000/month covers only a fraction of basic living costs and is completely disconnected from a worker's former industrial earnings.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

### 11.3 Economic and Moral Justification for the Policy Reform
The transition from fragmented assistance and employer liability to the NEIIS provides three decisive advantages:
1. **Poverty Shock Prevention:** Transforms vulnerable working-class households from potential recipients of generational safety nets into self-reliant, pension-insured families.
2. **Enterprise Protection:** Shields small and medium enterprises (SMEs) from catastrophic liquidation liabilities when industrial accidents occur.
3. **Global Trade Competitiveness:** Post-LDC graduation requires Bangladesh to comply with international trade standards (EU GSP+, US trade preferences). Ratification of ILO C121 through NEIIS provides verifiable proof of worker safety compliance, securing Bangladesh's export leadership in global markets.
